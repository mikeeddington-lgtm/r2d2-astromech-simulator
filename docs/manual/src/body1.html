Builder's manual

R2-D2 Astromech Simulator

Build your droid in a browser before you wire a single servo — then use the same browser to set the real servos up, choreograph them, and drive them.

1 · What this is, and what it is not

This is one HTML file. You download it, you open it, it works. No installation, no build tools, no account, no internet connection — everything, including the 3D model and the fonts, is inside the file. If it is on a USB stick it works from the USB stick.

What it does that a normal animation does not: it takes the real firmware sketches — the ones you would actually flash onto the droid — and runs them against a model of the hardware they expect. Sabertooth and Syren motor drivers, PCA9685 expanders, Pololu Maestro servo controllers, DY-SV5W and MD-YX5300 sound boards, an Xbox 360 receiver. When a panel opens on screen, it opens because the sketch told a modelled board to open it.

That distinction is the whole point, and it earns its keep: several real firmware bugs have been found here before they ever reached a droid — a dome-automation collision, an inverted volume convention, an integer map() truncation. A simulator that models the boards honestly will disagree with a sketch that is wrong.

The one thing to understand before you start

The model approximates; it never matches. On screen, a channel sitting at its min is drawn shut and at its max is drawn fully open — whatever the actual microsecond numbers are, and whichever way round they run. So a servo with a 200 µs span, a 700 µs offset, or a reversed linkage all animate the same clean full sweep.

The real board always gets the microseconds you measured. The model only ever gets "shut" and "open". This is deliberate: it stops an awkward linkage on your droid from making the picture on screen lie to you. Do not expect the on-screen panel angle to match your droid's.

Who this manual is for

Two people, often the same person on different days:

You have no hardware yet

Use it to decide what to buy, learn the control map, and find out what a build actually involves before you spend money. Chapters 1–4 and 10–12.

You have a board on the bench

Use it as the setup tool: find where each panel really stops, name the channels, choreograph them, and drive the real servos over USB. Chapters 5–16.

2 · Open it

  1. Download R2D2-Simulator.html from the latest release.
  2. Open it in Chrome or Edge. Double-clicking the file is enough.
  3. It opens on the setup wizard. Work through it, or press SKIP THE REST — nothing here is locked in and every answer can be changed later.
Why Chrome or Edge

Everything except one feature works in any modern browser. The exception is talking to real hardware over USB, which uses Web Serial — and Web Serial only exists in Chromium-based browsers. Firefox and Safari will run the simulator perfectly and simply will not offer the Connect hardware button.

If a card says your answers disagree

A sketch that cannot drive the board you picked says so, in plain words, on the Firmware card — and offers you one click to fix it. It never silently swaps your sketch. A fresh install ships with a deliberate disagreement in it (the default sound board and the default sketch do not know about each other) so you see what an objection looks like the first time you get there.

4 · Drive it

Close the wizard and you land on the Drive workspace: the droid on a stage, the pad along the bottom, and the sketch's own control map down the right.

{{CLIP:drive|START arms the feet; then drive, turn, spin the dome, open the pies, put the utility arms out, light the holoprojectors, and disarm. The keyboard mirrors the pad throughout.}}
Nothing drives until you press START

Both firmware families boot disarmed, and so does this. Press START (Enter) to arm the feet. The dome is on its own controller and is always live, armed or not — which is exactly how it behaves on the real droid.

If your real droid's feet ever move before you press START, something is wrong with your build.

Learn to drive

The LEARN tab on the right rail is a set of thirteen lessons, one at a time, each with a "how" and a "why", and each detecting for itself when you have done it. They are worth an afternoon — several of them exist because the thing they teach is a bug that has bitten a real build:

  • Open the dome — tap RT+▲, do not hold. A held d-pad restarts the sequence every couple of milliseconds and it never gets past its first frames. That is the bug this simulator found.
  • Turn it down — on the Maestro sketches the volume is backwards, because the constant was inherited from a board where 0 was loudest.
  • Let it run itself — on both Maestro sketches an automated dome turn blocks the whole loop for 750 ms. The HUD flags it while it happens.
  • Put it to sleep — finish every session disarmed. On the 2022 BETA a controller dropout does not disarm, so the droid can re-arm itself on reconnect.

Somewhere to drive

The stage buttons under the model: FOLLOW chases the droid, GRID puts a floor reference down, BG cycles the environments (studio, workshop, desert, hangar deck), TRACK lays out a practice circuit with gates and a timing line, and EDIT opens the track builder if you want your own lap. STUDIO and LEARN switch the right rail.

Handing the laptop to someone else

Sim only (Menu) is a public driving mode. It hides everything except the droid and the pad, and asks you to set a temporary password on the way out, so you can let a queue of people at a con drive your droid on screen without them finding the servo config.
