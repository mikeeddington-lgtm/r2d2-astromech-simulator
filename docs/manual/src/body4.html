16 · Files in and out

Everything this app reads and writes, in one table. The rule of thumb: its own formats carry everything; other people's formats carry what their format has room for, and anything that cannot cross the boundary is named rather than silently dropped.

FormatInOutCarries
servo config .jsonyesyes Names, travel, speed, acceleration and which part each channel drives.
whole-setup .jsonyesyes All of the above plus the build answers — the entire session in one file.
Pololu .mstr / .xmlyesyes A Maestro settings file. Opens in Control Center. Carries channels and sequences.
servos.hyesyes The PCA9685 channel table, as a C header you compile with.
sequences.hyesyes The routines, as a C header for the MaestroPCA library.
choreography .jsonyesyes The sequence library, bricks and all.

The round trip

Export, wipe the browser's storage, reload, rebuild the droid, import — and the frames come back target-for-target identical on every route. That is a tested property, not a hope.

Two things ride along inside the files, in comments that Control Center and the compiler both ignore: the brick structure and the part mapping. So a routine you exported comes back as bricks you can edit, and a channel you wired to a panel comes back wired to the same panel.

Somebody else's .mstr cannot carry your wiring

A Pololu <Channel> is a name, a mode, travel, speed and acceleration — nothing else. There is no field for "which panel is this?". A file from another builder therefore has to have its mapping guessed from the channel names, and if their numbering disagrees with the model's, some channels will land on the wrong part. The app tells you which; fix them on the Panels step or the dome map.

Frames back into bricks

A Pololu file carries poses and nothing else — no bricks. The app can work them out: each channel's curve is cut into excursions, each excursion becomes one rise-hold-fall brick, and then the proposal is measured against the original at every instant the file had an opinion about.

Two doors, and they do different things:

Work out the bricks

Applies it and keeps it. Use this when the file came from here in the first place, or when the routine is simple.

Work them out and review…

Applies it but holds the original alongside, flags every channel the bricks do not reproduce on the timeline, and shows the error live while you drag a brick until it says matches.

Both keep your original frame list in the library as "<name> (frames)". Accepting a guess changes what the droid does, so the thing it was guessing at has to survive. Discarding restores the frame list byte for byte.

17 · When nothing moves

Every entry here cost somebody a bench session. They all look identical from the outside — "nothing works" — and they all have a specific tell.

The one diagnostic that settles "is the Maestro receiving?"

Watch channel 0's Target box on Control Center's Status tab while the Arduino sends a setTarget. That box reads the board's own internal register, so it is independent of servo power, the servo, the linkage and the script. Target moves ⇒ the byte arrived. Target dead still ⇒ it did not. Reach for this first; almost every other test is ambiguous.

Servos

SymptomCause
Two servos fine, three judder and stall V+ supply sag, nine times out of ten — aggravated by channels left at speed 0 / acceleration 0, which makes every move a full-torque lunge. See chapters 7 and 8.
Channels go dead until you reconnect A deep sag reset the PCA9685. It lost its prescaler. Power problem.
The dial turns, the panel stops moving On a Maestro: the board is silently clamping to a stored limit. The app names it. Fix in Control Center.
One board's channels do nothing Its address is not what the sketch assumed. Read the scan line the sketch prints.
A write moves every servo The All Call address 0x70 got treated as a board. See chapter 14.
Channel 70 moves the servo on channel 6 Old bridge firmware, six-bit channel field. Re-flash.

Boards and serial

SymptomCause
Serial output chopped mid-sentence, one corrupt byte, then silence The DY-SV5W player dropped Serial to 9600. DY::Player defaults to Serial and its begin() calls Serial.begin(9600). Looks exactly like a hang inside player.begin(). Either watch the monitor at 9600 or construct the player on another port.
Nothing at all, not one byte, no clue anywhere The stock Padawan Usb.Init() failure path is a bare while(1); with the diagnostic print commented out. A bad USB Host Shield halts in setup() and loop() never runs.
A freshly factory-reset Maestro ignores its RX pin It comes up in USB Dual Port mode, which ignores the RX pin by design. Any test on a reset board is meaningless until Serial Settings are applied.
Wire on the Mini Maestro's third serial pin does nothing TXIN is a daisy-chain input used only in USB Chained mode, and dead in UART mode. A wire on it is indistinguishable from a dead board.
The yellow LED double-flashes That is normal — "at least one servo enabled or output driven high". Single flash means no servos enabled. It is a status indicator, not a fault code.
Multimeter at the Arduino end reads 5 V either way Proves less than it looks. A broken wire and an internally open input are identical from that side. Measure at the Maestro's own RX pad.

Proving a Maestro's RX pin is alive — no Arduino, no second board

  1. Serial Settings → USB Dual Port → Apply. In this mode the TTL Port's COM port is wired straight through to the physical RX and TX pins.
  2. Jumper the Maestro's own TX to its own RX.
  3. Open the TTL Port — not the Command Port — at 9600 in Pololu's Serial Transmitter and send a byte.

Byte returns ⇒ RX is alive. Nothing ⇒ RX is dead. Run a known-good board first as a control.

The panels on screen are all standing half open

That was a real bug, fixed in v1.46.1, and the fix is worth knowing as a principle: a channel's stored "home" or "neutral" microseconds are not a pose. Shut and open come from the directed pair — min and max, in that order — and nothing sorts them. If you see it again on a newer version, that is what regressed.

19 · What is saved, and where

Two stores in your browser, kept deliberately separate:

StoreHolds
PreferencesTheme, scale, your build answers, the RC map, the track, the Model Builder, paint, and the sequencer's brick colours.
Servo configThe channel table — names, endpoints, part mapping, speeds — and the sequence library.
Back it up as a file

Browser storage is per-browser and per-machine, and clearing site data clears it. An afternoon of measuring is worth thirty seconds of export: Finish in the servo setup tool offers the whole thing as one file. Keep it with your build notes.

Reset (Menu) really does clear the servo config as well as the preferences — it is a full reset, not a cosmetic one.

20 · Glossary

TermMeans
BrickOne part moving on the sequencer timeline: it opens, holds, closes. Bricks compile to frames.
FrameA pose — every channel's target at one instant. What the board actually plays.
ChannelOne servo output. Numbered per board and pin.
Part / actA moving thing on the model — a pie panel, a door, an arm. A channel drives a part.
Directed pairA channel's min and max in the order given: min is shut, max is open. Never sorted. Reversing a linkage is swapping them.
SlotOne of the eight subroutine numbers the droid's Arduino can call — restartScript(0..7).
Quarter-µsThe Maestro's own unit for a target. This app has spoken it since its first import, which is why talking to a real Maestro needed no conversion.
All CallI2C address 0x70, which every PCA9685 answers by default. A phantom board that is really all the boards.
Grey brickA brick for a part with no channel yet. Moves the model, compiles to nothing, starts driving the moment you map it.
