5 · Which servo moves what

A channel number means nothing until something is on the end of it. The Panels job (in the setup rail) is where you say which servo moves which part: one channel per part, and moving a part to a new channel frees the one it was on. The droid is beside you the whole time — press ▶ to prove the travel before you believe the mapping.

The same job is reachable three other ways, because you will want it from wherever you are standing: the drives column in the channel table, the Assign panels… button on the Board pane, and the dome map.

{{IMG:panels|The Panels job — every moving part on the droid, and the channel that moves it. The droid is beside you; ▶ proves the travel.}}

The dome map

Inside the servo setup tool, dome map… gives you a top-down drawing of the dome. Click the panel; the selected channel takes it. That is faster and far less error-prone than hunting a dropdown, because you are pointing at the thing rather than remembering its name.

{{CLIP:domemap|The map counts what is placed and what is not, and next unmapped walks you through the ones that are left. A panel that already has a channel is not blocked — clicking it moves the mapping, and frees the channel it was on.}}
Rotate it to match your droid

The map has a rotation slider sitting directly under the drawing it turns, 0–359°, and it remembers where you left it. Turn the drawing until it is oriented the way you stand when you work on your droid — every label stays upright as it turns.

Your numbering is not the model's numbering

Builders number panels their own way. If you call a channel "Panel7" and it drives what the model calls panel5, that is completely fine — but it means the name is not the mapping. The app keeps the mapping as real data and carries it through exports, so a file you wrote comes back wired the way you wired it. A file from somebody else, or a plain Pololu .mstr, carries no mapping at all and has to be guessed from the names — see chapter 16.

6 · The servo setup tool

This is the workbench, and it is the part of a build that takes the longest. Open it from Setup ▸ Servo setup ▸ Open the servo setup tool, or from the Board workspace. It opens already carrying everything you answered in the wizard — your controller, your expanders, your channel count.

Six steps across the top:

StepWhat it asks
1 ControllerWhich board is running the bench sketch — Nano, Uno, Mega, Mega ADK, ESP32. It only asks because the answer changes the I2C pins it draws and whether there is a spare hardware UART.
2 PCA9685sHow many boards. One question; chained-or-star, power routing and supply amps are behind ADVANCED, where the wiring diagram can still read them.
3 WiringThe loom for what you just described. Control signals only — every V+ line is deliberately left off, because power distribution is your call.

6 FinishHands you the file of everything you measured.

Step 5 — the channels

The list is who each channel is: use · # · board·pin · name · drives · test. Click a row and the Configure panel below follows it, holding everything else about that channel — its ends, its centre, reversed, boot behaviour, speed, acceleration, ease, sleep, the dial and the live drive.

  • use — tick the pins that have a servo on them. The count at the top is honest about how many of how many.
  • name — what you call it. Yours, not the model's.
  • drives — which part on screen it moves. This is the mapping from chapter 5.
  • test — opens and closes the channel off the directed pair, so a reversed channel really shuts. Press it to find out which panel you are actually looking at.
{{IMG:channels|The Channels step. A list of who each channel is, and one Configure panel that follows whichever row you click.}}
Bulk edits

Tick the left-hand column on several rows and the Apply bar at the bottom sets one setting — speed, acceleration, ease — on all of them at once. The button says "apply this setting to all N selected" so you know what you are about to do.

7 · Finding the ends

This is the heart of it. A servo's endpoints are a property of your linkage, not of the datasheet — so you find them by turning a dial with the panel in front of you, and record where it actually stops.

{{CLIP:bench|Pick a channel, turn the dial until the panel is where you want it, and press Set MAX. Turn it the other way and press Set MIN. The three numbers in the Configure panel and on the dial are the same three numbers throughout.}}
  1. Select the channel. The dial is not a mode you enter — it is simply there for whichever row you click.
  2. Turn the dial until the panel is exactly shut. Press Set MIN.
  3. Turn it the other way until the panel is exactly as far open as you want it — which is usually not as far as the servo can go. Press Set MAX.
  4. Set centre if the part needs a rest position that is not either end (a gimbal, not a door).
  5. Give it speed and acceleration. This matters more than it looks — see the warning below.
  6. Move to the next channel. Leaving means keeping: moving channel or closing the bench commits a staged end; cancel is the real undo.
{{IMG:dial|The dial, with the three ends under it. The tick marks on the dial are min, centre and max — where they sit is where your linkage actually stops.}}
Three things the dial deliberately does not do

Opening it does not move a servo. Clicking down a list would otherwise walk every panel on your droid to mid-travel in turn.

It does not permanently widen the channel. The dial can reach a little past your recorded endpoints (1000–2000 µs, a safe range that stops a horn hitting a hard stop) for exactly one move at a time — so a later save can never overwrite the travel you measured with the range you were searching in. If you genuinely need the full sweep, tick Advanced.

It does not hide a pending change. The panel and the dial show the same three numbers, marked not written yet when they differ from what is stored.

Leave speed and acceleration at zero and you will blame the wrong thing

A channel with speed 0, accel 0 means unlimited, and the engine takes that literally: it jumps straight from where it is to where you asked, in one step. On screen that looks fine. On real hardware it is a full-torque lunge on every servo at once, and the inrush stacks at exactly the same instant.

This is the single most common cause of "two servos are fine, three judder and stall". Give the channels real numbers — speed 80 / acceleration 10 is a proven starting point — and the peak current drops enormously.

8 · The bench, physically

None of the above helps if the electricity is wrong. Before you measure anything:

  1. Power the servo boards from their own 5–6 V supply. The PCA9685's V+ rail is the servo rail. It does not come from the Arduino, and it must not.
  2. Join the grounds. The co-processor, the expanders, the servo supply and the droid's Arduino all share 0 V. Everything else in this manual assumes you have done this.
  3. Budget the current. Roughly 1 A per small servo and 2–3 A per MG996R-class servo that is moving at the same time. Not per servo installed — per servo moving.
  4. Put a fat electrolytic across V+/GND at the board — 1000–4700 µF. This is not optional on a multi-servo droid.
  5. Flash the bench sketch (PCA_Bridge), plug the board in over USB, and press Connect hardware on the Channels step.
A deep sag can reset the PCA9685

If the supply dips far enough the chip loses its prescaler and its outputs simply stop until it is reconfigured. The tell is channels going dead until the app reconnects — connecting re-sends the clock configuration and a full sync. If you see that, it is a power problem, not a software one. Put a multimeter on V+ during a three-servo open and watch it.

Not connected is still useful

Without hardware the dial drives the on-screen model only, and the bench says so plainly at the top of the step. You can lay out the whole channel table, name everything and map every panel before a single board arrives — then connect and measure the real travel later.

9 · A config you already have

If you have set your servos up before, do not do it again. The Servo setup step's first question is exactly this, and the left-hand card takes:

  • a servo config .json — this app's own export;
  • a PCA9685 servos.h — the header you compiled from;
  • a Pololu .mstr or .xml — a Maestro settings file;
  • a whole-setup .json — everything, including the build answers.

An import brings the travel only by default — names, min, centre, max, speed and acceleration — and leaves your board, your sequences and your panel mapping exactly as they are.

Three visible choices, and it says what it will replace

When a file could bring more than one thing, you pick: servo config only · servo config and choreography · choreography only. Each names what it touches and what it leaves alone, and a choice the file cannot satisfy is greyed out with the reason. If it is about to replace work, it says so first and offers cancel · save a copy first · replace — and "save a copy" writes a timestamped file that must land before the import proceeds.
