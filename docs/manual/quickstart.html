Your first hour with the simulator

R2-D2 Astromech Simulator · quick start · one page

0 · Get it open

  1. Download R2D2-Simulator.html from the project's latest release.
  2. Double-click it. Chrome or Edge — everything works elsewhere except talking to real hardware over USB, which only Chromium browsers can do.
  3. No install, no internet, no account. It runs from a USB stick.

1 · Tell it what your droid is

It opens on the setup wizard: nine questions, each with pictures of the actual boards. Answer what you know and press SKIP THE REST for the rest — every answer applies straight away and nothing is locked in. The one that matters most is Servo hardware: Pololu Maestro or PCA9685 expanders. Almost everything downstream follows from it.

Firmware is asked last, on purpose

The sketch has to fit the hardware, not the other way round. If your answers disagree, the card says so in plain words and offers one click to fix it — it never swaps your sketch silently.

2 · Drive it (10 minutes)

Press Enter — that is START, and nothing drives until you press it. Then:

WASDdrive and turn (left stick)
J Lspin the dome — always live, armed or not
Rdrive speed 1‑2‑3
C+↑dome pie panels open (tap, don't hold)
Q+→utility arms out · F holoprojectors
Enterdisarm — finish every session disarmed

Then work down the thirteen lessons on the LEARN tab. Several exist because the thing they teach is a bug that has bitten a real build.

3 · Then the servos (the long part)

Setup ▸ Servo setup. Its first question is whether you already have a config — if you do, import it and skip everything below.

  1. Power the boards properly first. Own 5–6 V supply, common ground, big capacitor. See the bench card.
  2. Flash PCA_Bridge, plug the board in over USB, press Connect hardware.
  3. Tick the pins that have a servo on them. Name each one. Press test to find out which panel you are actually looking at.
  4. Click a row: the dial appears for that channel. Turn it until the panel is exactly shut → Set MIN. Turn it until it is as open as you want → Set MAX.
  5. Give every channel a speed and an acceleration — 80 and 10 is a proven start.
  6. Finish hands you the whole thing as one file. Keep it with your build notes.
Do not leave speed at 0

0 means unlimited, and the engine takes it literally: every move becomes a full-torque lunge, and the inrush of several servos stacks at the same instant. This is the number one cause of "two servos are fine, three judder and stall".

4 · Make it dance

SEQUENCE workspace. ＋ New sequence, then drag parts onto the timeline — or use Ready-made: pick a set of panels, pick a shape, and the whole figure lands at once. ▶ PLAY previews it. Then BOARD ▸ Build your sequences.h and choose which routines land on slots 0–7 — those eight subroutine numbers are the entire interface between the droid's Arduino and its servo controller.

Saving is not loading

Save keeps a routine in your library. Only Build your sequences.h puts it on the board. A faded chip in the library is one that is not loaded.

The one thing to understand

The model approximates; it never matches

On screen a channel at its min is drawn shut and at its max is drawn fully open — whatever the real numbers are and whichever way round they run. The real board always gets the microseconds you measured; the model only ever gets "shut" and "open".

So do not expect the on-screen panel angle to match your droid's — that is deliberate.

Use at your own risk

No warranty, of any kind. This models hardware — it cannot see your wiring, your supply or your linkage. Check what you are about to do before you do it, keep the power switch in reach, and verify every figure on your own bench. Your boards, your servos and your fingers are your responsibility.
