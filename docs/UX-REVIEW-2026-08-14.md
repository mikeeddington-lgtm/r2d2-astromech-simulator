# UX review — setup to sequences, as a first-time user

*2026-08-14 · against the built `R2D2-Simulator.html` v1.39.4 · fresh state (no saved prefs), walked in a clean Chromium from first load through the wizard, the workshop, servo setup and the sequencer. Every claim below was seen on screen and, where it mattered, confirmed in the source. Screenshots in `docs/shots/ux-2026-08-14/`.*

The brief: run through the sim knowing nothing, and report the friction, the duplication and the parts that add no value — then say what to change and why.

---

## 1. What already works — do not touch

Credit first, because the last month's simplification programme shows. The model-first wizard with three picture cards is immediately understandable. "Every answer applies straight away, and nothing here is locked in" is exactly the right promise, and **Skip the rest** means the wizard never holds you hostage. The honest badges (NOT SIMULATED, STANDS IN, NOT WORKING YET) build trust. The servo-setup step explains *why* the job exists before asking you to do it, and the three-things-that-break-servos warning is the best paragraph in the app. In the sequencer, the empty-routine hint ("Drag a part up from the library below — it makes its own lane") is exactly what a lost user needs, the library's click-shows-a-card-never-opens rule prevents accidents, and the ready-made shapes give a beginner a first success in one click. The lessons list is well written. None of the findings below argue with any of this.

## 2. The first five minutes — friction

### 2.1 Nothing on screen tells you to arm the feet ⚠ top finding

The first thing every new user does is push a stick or press W. Nothing moves. The only places that explain why are a dim line in the serial console ("— press START to arm the feet —"), which is on a tab the user has not found yet, and lesson 1 of a Learn mode they have not opened. The top-bar chip says DRIVE OFF but is passive — clicking it does nothing — and no hint appears when input arrives while disarmed.

The behaviour itself is right — both firmware families boot disarmed, and the sim should too. The *silence* is the bug.

**Change:** when a drive input arrives while disarmed, show a one-line hint by the pad or HUD — "Feet are disarmed — press START (Enter) to arm" — and make the DRIVE OFF chip a button that arms/disarms. Lesson 1 already has the wording.
**Why:** this is the single most likely first dead-end, and the fix is a toast plus one click target. A user who bounces here never reaches anything else you built.

### 2.2 Finishing setup drops you into silence

"FINISH — TAKE ME TO MY DROID" lands in the workshop with no suggestion of what to do next. The user has just answered nine questions and been promised a droid; the app should hand them a first move.

**Change:** after a *first-run* finish only, show one small card with three doors: **Drive it** (press START to arm) · **Learn to drive** (opens the existing lessons) · **Build a sequence** (opens the desk). All three doors already exist — this is one card, shown once.
**Why:** "what do I do now?" is the question every walkthrough of this kind died on. The wizard's last words should answer it.

### 2.3 On a genuine first run, every question already reads as answered

A brand-new user's step 1 shows the whole rail green-ticked: ✓ Controller, ✓ Servo hardware, ✓ Firmware… before they have touched anything (screenshot `01-first-load.png`). Those are the defaults for *this* droid — sensible — but a tick asserts "this was answered", so the rail says "already done" on step 1, and the user cannot tell what they chose from what was assumed.

**Change:** two chip states — a hollow marker for "assumed, not visited" and the ✓ only once the user has visited or changed the step. Alternatively, one honest first question: "This is the standard MK4 setup — keep it, or set up your own?"
**Why:** the ticks currently spend the trust the NOT SIMULATED badges earn. A tick should mean the same thing everywhere: *you* did this.

### 2.4 Fifteen steps reads as homework

"Step 1 of 15" undersells the truth: nine are questions, and six (Servo setup, Wiring, Panels, Colours, Scene, Finish) are jobs or browsers you can return to any time. The count alone is intimidating, and the rail draws all fifteen alike.

**Change:** keep every step, but split the rail visually — "Your hardware" (the nine questions) and "Jobs — come back any time" (the rest) — and number only the questions ("Question 4 of 9").
**Why:** collapse, don't hide — applied to the wizard's own frame. The work does not change; the *perceived* size halves.

### 2.5 Small first-session snags

- Clicking a part chip in the sequencer does nothing (drag-only), and beginners click first. A dead click reads as broken. **Change:** click adds the part's brick at the playhead; drag keeps working.
- BRICKS / POSE / FRAMES (editor modes) and HEAD / BODY / FULL (camera framings) sit in one row, styled identically. Two different kinds of control should not dress the same. **Change:** separate the groups, label the second "View".
- Leaving the sequencer restores the paint but not the camera — you come back to the workshop still zoomed into the dome. **Change:** restore the framing on exit exactly as `BLK.tint` is dropped.
- The inspector's idle text says "Click a brick to set how long it runs" even when the routine has no bricks (or is a frame list). **Change:** state-appropriate hint — empty routine gets "drag a part in", frame list gets the existing amber-card wording.
- A first new routine is auto-named "Routine 9" (numbering runs over the eight imported ones). **Change:** number from what the user can see ("Routine 1"), or name it "Untitled".

## 3. Duplication

### 3.1 One tool, two names, three blue buttons — the Servo setup step

The wizard's Servo setup step (screenshot `step-10b-bottom.png`) carries **Open the servo setup tool** (in the "No — measure them now" card), **Open the bench** (in the numbered procedure, which even says "Press Open the bench below" while the button above calls it something else), and **Export the servo config** — which the page itself admits is "the same file the bench writes from save servo config on its Finish step".

**Change:** one primary door, one name. Call the tool what the step is called — "Open the servo setup" — everywhere on the page, and drop the standalone export (the tool's own Finish step owns that, and since v1.38.3 it prompts when there is work to save).
**Why:** two names teach a beginner there are two tools; three primary buttons make the one required action a guess. This is the project's own v1.38.3 lesson ("two choices side by side assert they are equals") one page later.

### 3.2 The Maestro pane lists the same eight sequences twice

In the Bench view (screenshot `27-bench-view.png`), SEQUENCES (slot badges, rename, delete) sits directly above SCRIPT LOADOUT ("8 of 9 on the board") — the same eight names, twice, on one pane, with subtly different powers.

**Change:** merge into one list: each row carries its name, duration, slot/loaded state and a ▶; the loadout *editing* stays where it lives, in the builder.
**Why:** the library-is-not-the-board distinction is real and worth keeping — but it is a distinction between *surfaces*, not something one pane should demonstrate by repeating itself.

### 3.3 One thing, three nouns: routine / sequence / library

The sequencer says **+ New routine** and groups "Routines"; the Maestro pane says **+ Sequence**; the builder says "Your library" and compiles "sequences"; the save caption mentions "the Maestro script" on a build that has no Maestro. Four surfaces list the same objects under three vocabularies, each with its own create/rename/delete verbs.

**Change:** pick one noun — **sequence** (it is what `.mstr` and `sequences.h` both burn in) — and use it on every surface: "+ New sequence", library groups "On the board (8) / Library only (1)". Put rename/delete in one home (the library card) and let the other surfaces deep-link to it.
**Why:** a beginner cannot know "routine" and "sequence" are the same object; every synonym is a fork in their mental model.

### 3.4 Two controls for one state, and a place called "workshop" that nothing else names

The top-bar SEQUENCE button and the bottom SEQUENCER tab switch the same state — two controls, one door. And the desk's exit is "↩ BACK TO WORKSHOP", a name that appears nowhere else; the top bar calls that place DRIVE. Similarly, the top-bar **BENCH** view is not the servo bench — the tool the setup calls "the bench" is a different full-page popout.

**Change:** the top bar owns the mode names; the bottom tab row mirrors them exactly; the exit button says "← Back to Drive". Rename the BENCH *view* (it is the Maestro/outputs side of the app — "Board" would be honest) so "bench" means only the servo tool.
**Why:** the words are the map. Two names for one place, and one name for two places, both cost a beginner real minutes.

### 3.5 Deliberate duplication that should stay

The setup file's three doors (Menu, Config ▸ Files, wizard Finish), panel assignment's six doors, Learn's two doors — these follow the standing rule "a feature only reachable from the setup overlay is a feature nobody finds", and every one answers a "where do I…?" that was actually asked. Keep them; just spell the label identically at each door (e.g. always "Setup file — save & load").

## 4. Things that say the wrong thing — worse than no value

### 4.1 Bricks on parts with no servo silently vanish from the board ⚠ top finding

The parts strip offers every dome part — Pie 1–5, the *fixed* "Dome pie 6", and all fourteen side panels, which on this build have **no channel assigned** (the Panels step shows them all "—"). Drag them in, press Play, and the model performs beautifully. But `blockCompile()` resolves each brick through `blockChan()`, and a brick whose part has no channel contributes nothing — the compiled frames the board would play simply omit them. Nothing anywhere says so; the inspector's only note is about the loadout. (Screenshots `23-new-routine.png`, `21-sequence-view.png`.)

A first-timer builds a fourteen-panel Mexican wave, flashes the export, and on the real droid five pies twitch. The sim's core promise — what you preview is what you flash — breaks exactly where a beginner cannot diagnose it.

**Change:** grey the chip of any part with no channel and badge it ("no servo — assign in Panels", the badge being a door to the Panels step); and add a lint line in the builder: "14 of 14 parts in *Routine 9* have no servo assigned — they will play on the model only."
**Why:** v1.39.3 already established the principle for channels-without-parts; this is the same law facing the other way. The sim is allowed to animate them — it must just say what it is doing.

### 4.2 The builder's footer gives Pololu instructions on a PCA build — a bug

"Build your sequences.h" ends with: "The export writes your `<Channels>` back unchanged… At the bench: load it, **Apply Settings**, **Clear Errors** — and never press 'Copy all Sequences to Script'." That is the Maestro Control Center procedure. On a PCA build the export is a C header you compile into the MaestroPCA sketch — there is no Control Center, no `<Channels>`, no Copy-all button. In `maestro/builder.js` the script *preview* is correctly gated on `bldIsPca()`, but this hint is unconditional. v1.39.1 fixed exactly this class elsewhere ("on a PCA build the useful sentence is a different one") and missed this one.

**Change:** per-family footer. PCA: "sequences.h drops into your MaestroPCA sketch folder — recompile and flash the co-processor; slot numbers are `restartScript(n)`."
**Why:** it is not vague, it is *wrong*, and it is the last thing the user reads before touching real hardware.

### 4.3 Dead statistics in the PCA validate column

The same builder shows SCRIPT **0 / 0 B** and SUBROUTINES **0 / 126** on a PCA build — measurements of a machine that is not there (the v1.29.0 linter lesson, verbatim). Zeros read as "something is broken".

**Change:** on a PCA board, replace them with the two limits that bite — slots used / 128 and channels used / board pins — which the linter already computes.

### 4.4 The starter generator still shouts the warning the linter dropped

On first entry to the desk the console logs: "that script is roughly 1610 bytes and the PCA9685 ×2 holds 0 — trim sequences or move to a bigger board." v1.29.0 removed exactly this from the lint; it survives in `makeStarter()` (`maestro/starters.js`), and "trim sequences" is the wrong advice anyway on a build whose answer is "add a co-processor or play from the sim".

**Change:** gate the estimate on `boardIsPca()` the same way the linter does.

### 4.5 On the default build, the desk promises a board that is not there

The default MK4 answer is *PCA9685 straight off the droid's board* — the wizard card itself says "there is nowhere to store movements". Yet the desk says "BOARD: not on the board — ⚙ Build your sequences.h puts it there", the pane header says "the slot numbers below are what your sketch already sends" (mod2026 sends no `restartScript` on this topology), and the loadout badge says "8 of 9 on the board". The app contradicts its own wizard card.

**Change:** the desk should read the *topology*, not just the board family. On the no-co-processor arrangement: "This build has nowhere to store sequences — they play here, and on real servos via ⚡ live drive. To put them on the droid, add a MaestroPCA co-processor (Servo hardware step)." One sentence, one door.
**Why:** for the naive user this is the difference between "the app is lying" and "the app just told me what to buy next".

### 4.6 Jargon leaking through the basic layer

"sub Dome_Pies_Open" in the routine header, "113f", and the parts strip's raw CAD names — Panel13, Panel5, Panel12, in an order that looks random against the tidy Pie 1–5 beside them (it is the CAD's numbering, which the naming layer exists to hide). The rule is already written: lead with the label, CAD name in the tooltip.

**Change:** subroutine names move behind the Advanced switch; side panels get positional labels ("Side panel 1…14" by the same azimuth convention that fixed the pies); "113f · 5.0s" becomes "5.0s" at the basic level.

## 5. Priorities

| # | Change | Class | Cost |
|---|--------|-------|------|
| 1 | 4.2 builder footer per family | wrong instruction, by the project's own v1.39.1 standard | small |
| 2 | 4.1 unassigned-part chips greyed + lint line | sim-vs-real divergence, silent | medium |
| 3 | 2.1 disarmed hint + clickable DRIVE chip | first-session dead end | small |
| 4 | 4.3 + 4.4 dead stats and stale warning | zeros and false alarms | small |
| 5 | 2.2 first-run "what next" card | orientation | small |
| 6 | 4.5 topology-aware desk wording | self-contradiction | small–medium |
| 7 | 3.3 one noun for sequence/routine | vocabulary | medium (many strings, tests) |
| 8 | 3.4 one name per place (workshop/Drive, Bench view) | vocabulary | small |
| 9 | 3.1 one door on the Servo setup step | duplication | small |
| 10 | 2.3 assumed-vs-confirmed chips | trust | medium |
| 11 | 2.4 wizard rail grouped questions/jobs | perceived size | small |
| 12 | 3.2 merge pane's two sequence lists | duplication | medium |
| 13 | 2.5 + 4.6 polish set | friction/jargon | small each |

Items 7 and 12 touch strings and layouts that tests assert on (`sequencer-ui`, `workspaces`, `build-config`) — budget for test updates, and per the standing rule, prove any new assertion can fail before trusting it.

## 6. What this review did not cover

Real hardware (no board attached), the RC transmitter path, music, kiosk mode, the Anzellan head and Polar Mouse flows, and PCA Studio — all walked past, not through. The Maestro-family topologies (m1/m2c/m2s) were not exercised; 4.2/4.5 should be re-checked from a Maestro build's point of view when fixing, so the fix does not simply invert the bias.
