# UX review — 2026-08-22 (v1.68.0)

> **STATUS.** Four cold-start walkthroughs of the built app by people who had
> never seen it: a general first-timer, a hardware builder, someone trying to
> make a show, and one covering the remaining surfaces plus a naming audit.
> ~600 screenshots. **85 findings**, ranked below.
>
> **12 were code-level and are fixed in v1.69.0** — marked ✅ in place. The rest
> are interface decisions and are left for you to triage; nothing here was
> changed on your behalf.
>
> The single most useful sentence in this document: **the app is beautifully
> written and badly sequenced.** Its explanations are among the best I have read
> in a tool of this kind — and almost all of them appear at the exits rather
> than the entrances.

---

## If you only fix ten things

1. **Pushing the stick while disarmed produces total silence** (§1.1). Every new
   visitor's first action. Nothing happens and nothing says why.
2. **The setup wizard reopens at Question 1 on every page load, forever**
   (§1.2). Answering all nine and dismissing it does not count as consent.
3. **"Question 9 of 9" is not the end** — six more full screens follow (§1.3).
4. **Three channel maps disagree about which servo is on which pin** (§3.1).
   The one fact a hardware builder came for.
5. **Travel limits far outside the stated safe range are accepted, saved and
   exported with no warning at the point of entry** (§2.1). The servo-killer.
6. **Nine unlabelled export buttons**, one of which makes a file for the wrong
   board (§4).
7. **The Ready-made row — the one-click show builder — is below the fold**
   (§5.1). It turns a twenty-minute struggle into three seconds.
8. **Six words for one concept**, in a tool whose whole job is to explain
   hardware to beginners (§6).
9. **`Follow`, `Grid` and `Reset pose` are unreachable at 800×600** and hidden
   under the sequencer pane at the default size (§7.1) — the only way to recover
   when you lose the droid.
10. **The manual — the one signposted route out of confusion — opens a blank
    tab and says nothing** (§8.1).

# §1 The first fifteen minutes

**Time to first movement, honest first attempt: ~4½ minutes and four wrong
turns.** Mechanical minimum once you know the trick: 2 clicks and a held key.

### 1.1 Silence when you try to drive — BLOCKER
Hold `W`, or drag the on-screen stick, or drag the DRIVE slider. The dot under
the stick moves, so the app clearly *heard* you. `DRIVE` stays 0, the droid does
not twitch, and no message, toast, flash or tooltip appears anywhere.

The blocking fact — the foot motors are disarmed until you press Start — is
written in exactly one place, as the last three words of a table row
(`Left stick … Drive + turn (feet) — armed only`), and you must join it to a
*second* table (`Start / Back  ↵ / N`) to act on it. The `?` help card, the one
place you would look, has neither.

Every one of the four walkthroughs got stuck here. One of them assumed the app
was broken and closed it.

**Smallest fix:** when drive input arrives while disarmed, put one line over the
stage for three seconds — **"Feet are disarmed — press Enter (Start) to arm."**
Second-best: default to armed, since there is no real robot to run over.

*Related:* arming actually needs a **held** Start; a tap does nothing, and that
is documented nowhere.

### 1.2 The wizard reopens at Question 1 on every load — BLOCKER
Answer all nine questions, press **Skip the rest**, drive around, reload — and
the wizard is back at Question 1 of 9 with every chip showing ✓ and your answer
beside it. Close it, reload: Question 1 again. Escape, reload: Question 1 again.

The cause is one line: `main.js:291` opens the wizard when `!buildConfigured()`,
and `hardware.js:777` defines that as `PREFS.build.done` — which only the final
**Finish** job ever sets. `closeStartup()` sets `PREFS.seenStartup`, which is
read for the Escape key and not for the boot check.

This also caused §1.4 and it is why `CONFIGURE` says the droid is **"NOT SET UP
YET"** (§3.4) while listing all nine answers correctly.

**Smallest fix:** `if(!buildConfigured() && !PREFS.seenStartup) wizOpen(0);`

### 1.3 The progress counter lies — MAJOR
Reaching "Question 9 of 9" and pressing **Next →** lands you on *Servo setup*,
then *Wiring, Panels, Colours, Scene, Finish* — six more full screens, drawn as
a second row of identical chips under the same heading and counted by the same
footer.

**Fix:** make the last question's button read **Finish setup →** and land in the
app. The six jobs already say "come back any time"; reach them from their own
chips.

### 1.4 One stray click replaces your droid with a wheeled trolley — MAJOR
Because of §1.2 the wizard is open when you think you are in the app. A click in
the middle of the window to give the 3D view focus lands on the **Polar Mouse +
chariot** card. The droid is replaced, four questions grey out, and nothing is
said — you find out three screens later.

This is the most far-reaching change in the app (it rewrites the whole
configuration) and it is one accidental click with no confirmation and no
announcement. To the app's great credit, **undoing it worked perfectly** — one
click back to R2-D2 and everything restored.

**Fix:** a toast at the top of the wizard — *"Model changed to Polar Mouse +
chariot — [Undo]"*.

### 1.5 The wizard arrives *after* you have started using the app — MAJOR
The first thing painted is the complete app with a droid on the stage. Several
seconds later a full-screen questionnaire drops over it. The first impression is
"the app crashed into a form", and it steals a click if you were reaching for
something.

### 1.6 The last question ships a default its own screen says is broken — MAJOR
Q9 pre-selects `padawan360 mod2026`, which carries two red ✗ lines
("it cannot reach expanders that live behind a co-processor", "mod2026 drives an
MD-YX5300, not a DY-SV5W"), and a box below recommends something else. So the app
chose an answer, told you it was wrong, and left it selected — and **Next** is
the big blue button.

**Fix:** auto-select the recommendation and say *"We moved you to Maestro 2025
(PWM) because…"*. Never leave a ✗-flagged card marked `SELECTED`.

### 1.7 No "I'm not sure" where it matters most — MAJOR
Q7 (Foot drive) opens *"This is the open decision on your build"* and then offers
exactly two committing answers with no way to say you have not decided. Q4 asks
**"MAESTRO, OR PCA9685?"** — the exact word a beginner has only seen on a forum —
and its third option is `Something else`, badged `NOT SIMULATED`, which reads
like a punishment.

The app already does this well elsewhere (`No dome motor yet`, `None yet`), so
the pattern exists and is simply missing where it costs most: forced to pick,
a beginner picks wrong, and every later screen is built on that.

### 1.8 A whole question where every answer does nothing — MAJOR
Q6 (Dome lighting): all three options carry `NOT SIMULATED`, and the selected one
carries `NOT SIMULATED` **instead of** `SELECTED`, so you cannot tell it is
chosen. **Fix:** fold it into a one-line row on the Wiring job.

### 1.9 Driving forward loses the droid — MAJOR
The reward for finally solving §1.1 is that the droid leaves the frame and the
viewport goes blank white while `DRIVE` reads 87. `RESET POSE` recovers it — but
"pose" suggests it resets the *arms*, not the position. **Fix:** default
`FOLLOW` on; rename to **Recentre droid**.

### 1.10 The status chips are unreadable, and are secretly the buttons — MAJOR ✅
At the default 1440×900 every chip is cut mid-word: `VIRTUAL P_`, `DRIVE O_`,
`NO BOA_`. You cannot tell whether `DRIVE O_` means on or off — and it is the
one-click cure for §1.1, disguised as a passive readout with its label chopped
off. At 150% two chips showed no characters at all; at 1024×700 they became six
anonymous dots, two of them the same grey, none carrying a `title`.

**✅ Fixed in v1.69.0** with a measured tier ladder — full words, then compact
words, then a dot that keeps the Drive state, then bare dots, every one carrying
a `title` and an `aria-label`. `FEET OFF` / `FEET ARMED` now survives down to
800×600.

### 1.11 `BOARD` the tab and `NO BOARD` the chip — MAJOR
Two controls 300 px apart whose visible text is `BOARD` and `NO BOA_`. Clicks
aimed at the tab hit the chip, nothing visibly happens, and the tab appears
broken. **Fix:** rename the chip — it is about a *physical* board, the tab is
about the *sequence* board, and the collision is only in the wording.

### 1.12 Smaller, but they all cost a beginner something
- **"SKIP THE REST"** is the only fast way in, and it sounds like giving up. It
  loses nothing. Call it **Take me to the droid**.
- **Escape** closes the wizard and dialogs but does nothing on the `SEQUENCE`
  screen, the busiest and most disorienting in the app.
- **Four badge vocabularies for one state**: `ON THE STAGE` (Q1), `SELECTED`
  (Q2–Q8), nothing (Q6), and no badge at all on some cards. Nobody worked out
  whether `STANDS IN` meant "works" or "is faked".
- **Q4 has a page of content below the fold** with no scrollbar, arrow or fade.
- **The on-screen pad reads as a legend, not a control** — flat grey line art
  with 8 px labels. It is draggable and does drive; its face buttons do nothing,
  so half of it is live and half is decoration.
- **`MENU`** is a five-row settings popover. `Settings` would mislead nobody.
- **The plain untextured droid is shown as if it were the model** while the CAD
  loads. A one-line "Loading the CAD…" would cover it.

# §2 The hardware path

### 2.1 The safe range is displayed and not defended — MAJOR (the servo-killer)
The dial panel says **"safe range · 1000–2000 µs"**. Typing `2700` into Set MAX
and `2600` into Set MIN turns nothing red, warns nothing, and `save servo
setting` accepts it — producing a channel whose **centre sits outside its own
min–max** (`shut 2600 · centre 1000 · open 2700`). The exported `.mstr` carries
`min="10400" max="10800" neutral="6000"`. A red chip does appear — at the top of
the table, off-screen, using a *third* set of thresholds (500–2500), and only
after saving.

The bench's own text says it best: *"a horn held against a hard stop strips its
gears in seconds and does it quietly."* The app knows the rule and does not
enforce it.

**Fix:** turn the box red and disable `save servo setting` outside the safe
range or when centre falls outside min…max; require an explicit "my servo takes
this" tick. One threshold everywhere.

### 2.2 "Export anyway" is the primary button next to 129 errors — MAJOR
`ERRORS 129`, `WARNINGS 9`, and the same sentence repeated 129 times
un-deduplicated. The two footer buttons are `EXPORT ANYWAY (.MSTR)` — blue,
primary — and `DONE`. There is no "fix it" and no link to the offending channel.
The errors panel is also **amber, the same colour as the warnings panel**.

**Fix:** collapse to *"129 frames on 1 channel (ch 0 · Top left pie) exceed its
limits — [Fix channel 0]"*, make the fix the primary button, demote export, make
errors red.

### 2.3 "Connect hardware" does nothing at all — BLOCKER
`navigator.serial` is present; the button produces no port picker, no toast, no
error, no state change. Someone will start unplugging and reflashing hardware
that is fine.

**Fix:** catch the rejection and replace the banner text with the reason —
*"No board offered. Have you flashed PCA_Bridge? Is the port already open in
Maestro Control Center or the Arduino IDE? Chrome or Edge only."* Never let the
click produce nothing.

### 2.4 The servo bench for a Maestro build is entirely a PCA9685 bench — BLOCKER
With `SERVO HARDWARE = Pololu Maestro · Mini 24`, the only place in the app where
endpoints can be set opens a six-step wizard about PCA9685 expanders: *"What is
driving the PCA9685s?"*, *"How many PCA9685 boards?"*, PCA wiring, flash
`PCA_Bridge` — and then step 6 lurches back to *"Your Maestro's own settings
still come from Control Center"* under a summary reading `PCA9685s: 2 · 0x40,
0x41`. Four of six steps are about hardware the user was never asked to buy.

**Fix:** branch the bench on `servoHardware`.

### 2.5 One panel, two vocabularies, two "reverse" ticks, out of sync — MAJOR
The `Configure` strip (`shut / centre / open`, `reversed`) sits directly above
the dial panel (`Set MIN / Set CENTER / Set MAX`, `reverse`). Typing 1450 into
Set MIN moves the dial while the strip carries on saying `shut 1000`, with no
indication either is a draft. "shut/open" and "MIN/MAX" are not obviously the
same thing to a beginner — on a reversed panel "shut" *is* the max.

**Fix:** one vocabulary; make the strip a read-only summary while the dial is
open.

### 2.6 Half the edit persists and half does not — MAJOR
Rename a channel and set its `DRIVES`, reload: the name survived, `DRIVES`
reverted. No save button, no dirty indicator. Mapping twenty channels to twenty
parts is an afternoon's work, and it *looks* like it saved.
**✅ The underlying cause is fixed in v1.69.0** (`chAssign()` never called
`HW.save()` — code review §1.5), but the missing "saved" affordance remains.

### 2.7 The Maestro path hides the bench and sends you to other software — MAJOR
With Maestro selected, the "Servo setup" job offers two things: import a `.mstr`,
or go and measure everything in Pololu Control Center, stating flatly *"This
simulator does not drive one directly."* With PCA9685 selected, the same job
grows an **Edit the channel table** button that opens the bench. The bench exists
on the Maestro path too — two clicks away in a tab you have not found — and was
used all afternoon to set Maestro endpoints.

### 2.8 A PCA9685 header is offered, and produced, for a Maestro build — MAJOR
Enabled with `Mini 24` selected, producing a file whose comments read *"channel i
lives on PCA9685 board (i/16), pin (i%16)"*. No warning.

### 2.9 The dome map is the best idea in the app, and it is below the fold
A top-down dome, click a channel then click the panel it moves, with a running
"20 of 20 placed" count. It opens ~1700 px below the viewport, so the button
appears to do nothing. Rotating to 90° carries the `FRONT` label round and clips
it to "ONT"; the rotate slider has no caption saying what it is for.
**✅ The wizard's copy now honours the bench's saved rotation and has a rotate
control of its own** (code review §3.4); the scroll and the label are open.

### 2.10 "2 boards = 24 channels" beside a table that says 0–31 ✅
The stat printed the *loaded table's* size beside the number just typed, and the
generated sketch comment said `// 16 channels each, 24 in total`. **Fixed in
v1.69.0**, with the both-numbers sentence and the "grow the table" button the
Channels step already carries.

### 2.11 Choosing Maestro silently rewrote another answer ✅ (partly)
Clicking the Pololu Maestro card also moved Firmware to "Maestro 2025 (PWM)"
with no message; clicking back reverted the servo answer and **left the firmware
changed**. The footer promises "nothing here is locked in". **Fix:** say so
inline, with an Undo. *(The related pre-v1.36 mis-read of a saved build is fixed
in v1.69.0 — code review §3.2.)*

### 2.12 The wiring sheet cannot answer the question it exists for — MAJOR ✅ (partly)
The diagram is board-to-board and never shows a servo on a channel. The table
below covers the dome (19 rows) and lists **ten** body doors, arms, dataport,
chargebay and drawer under *"Rigged in the model but nothing drives them"* with
`not driven` in red. The `TRAVEL` column reads "set on the board" for every
channel and `INV` is empty, so the sheet carries none of the calibration just
done. The CSV and the HTML disagree about one row.

**✅ Fixed in v1.69.0:** the diagram drew a two-board build as **one board with
duplicated pin numbers** — the default build is a two-board build, so that was
the default output. It now draws one titled section per board.
**Open:** put the "10 of 29 parts have no channel" count on screen *before*
export, and fill TRAVEL/INV from the channel table.

### 2.13 The firmware answer never reaches the wiring artefacts — BLOCKER
The rail chip says `FIRMWARE — Maestro 2025 (PWM)`; the Wiring job's diagram
subtitle says `padawan360 mod2026 · padawan_secure _mode.ino` (note the stray
space), draws the arrow to the Maestro **dashed** = "this sketch does not talk to
it", and the exported file is named `R2-wiring-mod2026-….html` while its own
table inside says **Firmware: Maestro 2025 (PWM)**. One document contradicting
itself, on the page a builder prints and takes to the bench.

**Fix:** derive the diagram, its subtitle and the filename from the same
`firmware` value the chip shows.

# §3 Contradictions — the app disagreeing with itself

### 3.1 Three channel maps, live at the same time — MAJOR
Same build, same moment, three answers to "which servo is on which channel":

| Surface | Says |
|---|---|
| Board channel table | ch0–5 = Dome Pie 1–6, ch6–19 = Dome Panel 1–14. No body doors or arms. |
| OUTPUTS tab | `PCA9685 @ 0x41 · BODY` ch0 = *Left body door* … and `@ 0x42 · DOME PIES` with **11** dome pies |
| Wiring sheet | "Driven — 19 channels" (dome only), plus 10 body actuators as `not driven` |

And the Drive sidebar says `2× PCA9685 (0x40 body, 0x41 dome pies)` while OUTPUTS
says `0x41` and `0x42`.

**Fix:** one channel table rendered three ways. If OUTPUTS means "what the
sketch would emit" rather than "what to wire", say so in its heading.

### 3.2 The lesson counts contradict each other on one screen — MAJOR
*"The **thirteen** lessons below…"* / *"LESSONS **1 OF 12**"* / *"**1/12**"* on
the stage card / *"**21 CHAPTERS**"* in the header — and the prose says 13 + 20
chapters, which is 33, not 21. **Fix:** one source.

### 3.3 The library has eight sequences; the board has none
The `SEQUENCE` library shows eight named chips; `BOARD` says *"Nothing is on the
board yet · 0 of 0"*. The reconciling sentence is a 9 px footnote — *"A faded
chip is one that is not loaded"* — and the chips do not look faded. Visiting the
sequencer once populates the board, so the state is transient, which makes it
harder to understand rather than easier.

### 3.4 "NOT SET UP YET" under a complete list of your answers
The `CONFIGURE` tab lists all nine answers correctly and heads the section `NOT
SET UP YET` (same root cause as §1.2). It also carries a red **"These do not line
up"** block with three hardware conflicts and a `Sabertooth watchdog starvation`
warning — the most important thing the app knows about your build, in a tab found
by accident. **Fix:** surface conflicts as a banner on the Drive screen.

### 3.5 The receipts
- The Model Builder's import toast says **"loaded 0 part(s)"** in success
  styling while destroying the assembly (§5.5). ✅ *Fixed in v1.69.0.*
- Exporting produces a file with no visible confirmation — three exports in a
  row produced three files and no feedback anyone could reliably spot.
- The sound bank reports "53 of 53 tracks" from a count of decodes, not saves.
  ✅ *Fixed in v1.69.0.*
- A sequence's popover claims *"on the board as sub 8"* while a different
  sequence of the same name is what actually exported (§5.4).

# §4 The export maze

Nine file buttons live in one collapsed row (`▸ ALL 9 FILE BUTTONS, ONE BY ONE`)
with no labels of their own, plus more elsewhere.

| File | Doors | What it is | Can a beginner tell? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Setup `.json`** | Menu → Setup file → Save & load; Finish job | The whole configuration. **This is "save my work".** | No — buried in a Menu sub-dialog |
| **Servo config `.json`** | 3 buttons, 3 names | Travel only | No |
| **`.mstr`** | 3 buttons, 3 names | The file that goes on a Maestro | No — the one that matters is labelled "anyway" |
| **`sequences.h`** | Board; PCA Studio | Arduino header — **PCA9685 only** | No, and it is offered on the Maestro path |
| **Wiring sheet `.html`** | Wiring job; Board | Printable reference | Partly — it downloads rather than opening |
| **Wiring `.csv`** | one | Same table | Yes |
| **Maestro script** | Build your Maestro → Copy script | Text to paste into Control Center | No — nothing says where to paste it |
| **PCA Studio project `.json`** | PCA Studio | A fifth format, in a different app | No |

**Which two should a beginner press, and the app never says:** *Export setup
(.json)* to keep an afternoon's work, and *Export .mstr* to get something onto a
Maestro (then File → Open in Control Center → Apply Settings).

**Behind Advanced:** the PCA9685 header, Copy script, the three starter
generators, the CSV, and the two imports whose names do not distinguish them.
**Leave three visible:** Save my setup, Export .mstr, Wiring sheet.

The same collapsing applies to the three names for one file — `EXPORT SERVO
CONFIG` / `save servo config` / `EXPORT THE SERVO CONFIG` all write the same
thing.

# §5 Making a show

**Time and clicks to first animation: theoretical minimum 4 clicks and ~20
seconds; actual, honest: ~18 minutes and ~14 interactions, with the working
gesture found by accident.**

### 5.1 `READY-MADE` is below the fold — MAJOR
At 1440×900 the PARTS panel is clipped mid-sentence, and the clipped half-line
reads as the bottom of the panel rather than as "there is more". Underneath it,
invisible, sit `READY-MADE · pick a set, pick a shape`, a group picker and six
shape buttons. **One click on `MEXICAN WAVE` built the entire convention show.**

That is the feature that turns twenty minutes into three seconds, and the default
window size hides it.

**Fix:** put `READY-MADE` **above** the individual part chips, and use it in the
empty state: *"Start from a ready-made: [Wave] [Chase] [All at once] — or pick a
single part below."*

### 5.2 "Drag one onto the timeline" is the only documented gesture — MAJOR
Two careful drags produced nothing; a single **click** on the same chip added a
brick instantly. Later drags did work, so drag is not broken — but it is fragile
enough to fail a careful user, while the reliable gesture is never advertised.
The reverse hazard is also real: clicking a chip to find out what "Panel13" *is*
silently appends a brick.

**Fix:** *"click a part to add it at the end, or drag it where you want it."*

### 5.3 Nothing tells you your brick is on a part you cannot see — MAJOR
The first brick was `Panel1`, on the far side of the dome from the default
camera. Three presses of ▶ and nothing visibly moved. The honest answer the app
gave to "did the thing I made do anything?" was *no*.

**Fix:** tint the moving parts during playback (there is already a *Colour the
model to match* checkbox — make it the default while playing), and when a
selected brick's part is back-facing, offer *"Panel1 is on the far side —
[turn the droid round]"*.

Compounding it: adding a brick auto-zooms to that part and ends up **inside** the
geometry — a featureless grey surface — and every subsequent chip click hijacks
the camera again.

### 5.4 Two sequences with the same name; the wrong one exports — BLOCKER ✅
Saving under a name already in the library produced two identical chips,
distinguishable only by frame count. Adding the 31-frame one to the board put the
**5-frame** one there; the export contained the 5-frame routine; and the
31-frame one's popover still claimed *"on the board as sub 8"*.

**✅ Fixed in v1.69.0** — Save now offers Replace / Keep both, and a new
`seqUniqueName()` stops the two "new sequence" doors minting colliding names.
*(The Maestro-pane door and the music builder still need to call it — code
review §9a/9.)*

### 5.5 Importing the shipped example destroys your work and says "loaded" — BLOCKER ✅
`R2-model-simple-face.json` — one of the app's **own** examples — emptied the
stage and toasted **"loaded 0 part(s)"** in success styling, with no undo. The
other example, `R2-model-robot-arm.json`, imports fine, so the two ship files
behave completely differently. **✅ Fixed in v1.69.0**: validated before
committing, refuses with the unrecognised types named, and leaves the assembly
untouched. The example file itself is still a v2 format this build cannot read —
either withdraw it or add the part types.

### 5.6 Your show lands in slot 8, which nothing can trigger — MAJOR
Eight stock routines occupy slots 0–7. Yours goes to slot 8. `SLOTS USED 9`,
`ERRORS 0`, and the fine print says the stock sketches call `restartScript(0)–(7)`
from the d-pad. So you flash the droid and no button plays your show, while a big
green `ERRORS 0` says all is well. **Fix:** mark slots ≥8 as "not on a d-pad
button", count them as a warning, offer "move to top".

### 5.7 There is no way to delete a sequence — MAJOR
Every `+ New Sequence` click creates a permanent library entry, including empty
ones. One walkthrough finished with 21 entries. The popover offers Open, Preview,
Insert, Insert as one brick and a group field — **no rename, no duplicate, no
delete**. **Fix:** add both, and do not commit a new sequence to the library
until it has a brick.

### 5.8 You cannot put a sound anywhere in a show — MAJOR
"Panels open **and he beeps**" is the show. The PARTS list is mechanical parts
only. `♪ LOAD MUSIC` is a backing track to snap frames to, not a cue you can
place. The Drive view has a whole sound bank and the controller can fire sounds;
none of it is reachable from the timeline. **Fix:** a Sound lane — a bank/track
number dropped as a zero-length cue, firing in the sim and emitting a
`playSound(n)` line on export.

### 5.9 Dome rotation and drive are not in the sequencer either — MAJOR
The second thing on everyone's list, and there is no channel for it. **Fix:** at
minimum a greyed row saying *"Dome rotation — driven by the pad, not the
sequencer"*, so ten minutes of searching becomes one line of reading.

### 5.10 The mental model the sequencer demands at the door
Before you can make one panel move you must already understand:
1. a sequence lives in a **library**, separate from what is **on the board**,
   separate from the **timeline**, separate from the exported **file**;
2. **bricks vs frames** — two representations with a conversion between them,
   explained in a banner before you have made anything;
3. a **part** is a **channel** is a **lane**, and some parts have no channel;
4. **slots** — eight numbered subroutines wired to d-pad buttons.

All four could be deferred. The only thing needed to make a panel open and close
is: pick a part, say how long. Bricks-vs-frames matters when you open an imported
file; slots matter at export; library-vs-board matters when you press Build.

The app clearly knows how to teach — `CLEAR EVERY BRICK?` and `RESET EVERYTHING`
are models of the craft. It spends that skill on the exits rather than the
entrance.

### 5.11 The rest
- **Two length numbers 40 px apart**: header `3.5s`, summary `LENGTH 3.1s`. Also
  `1 bricks`, `1 frames` — no singular. *(The 400 ms discrepancy is ✅ fixed in
  v1.69.0 — code review §2.6.)*
- **The library search filter persists across reloads and the count lies** —
  `MY SEQUENCES (2)` is a filtered count presented as the real one, so a returning
  user appears to have lost 19 sequences.
- **After reload the app forgets which sequence you were editing** and reopens the
  wizard.
- **Ten greyed parts invite you to build a show that cannot run** — *"You can
  still drag them in and build the routine now"*, with no mark on the resulting
  bricks and no warning at export.
- **The timeline does not fit, does not grow and scrolls without telling you**;
  moving a brick re-anchors the ruler so the brick appears not to move while its
  start time changes.
- **The import dialog gives no sign it worked** — its own copy promises *"Nothing
  is written until the second one is answered"*, but the default answer is
  pre-selected, so choosing the file *is* the second answer. Eight routines
  arrived behind the still-open modal.
- **The file picker's `accept` contradicts what the dialog says it takes**, and
  the app apologises for it in prose: *"if the file you were sent is greyed out,
  choose 'all files'"*.

# §6 Naming — one concept, six words

Counted in source, all user-visible:

| Concept | Words in use | Recommend |
|---|---|---|
| A stored movement | **sequence** (245) · **routine** (180) · **script** (161) · **subroutine** (52) · **sub** · **animation** (13) · **frame list** · **macro** | **sequence** everywhere; keep `sub` inside generated code only |
| One servo output | **channel** (966) · **servo** (806) · **slot** (140) · **port** (114) · **pin** (108) · **actuator** (74) · **output** (38) | **channel** for the number, **servo** for the thing; retire slot/actuator/output from user text |
| Working out travel | **Servo setup** · **servo config** · **servo layout** · **servo gauges** · **the bench** · **servo setup tool** · **servo rack** · **servo table** | **servo bench** (the tool), **servo config** (the file) |
| Getting it onto the board | **Build your sequences.h** · **Build your Maestro** · **Build its Maestro layout** · **Build a servo layout** · **the ⚙ builder** | one verb: **Put on the board** |
| The parts editor | **Builder** (model) · **🔧 Build** (button) · **Builder pane** — the stage toolbar shows `BUILDER` and `🔧 BUILD` **side by side, meaning different things** | model = **Mechanism**; button = **Open mechanism editor** |
| The puppet head | **Anzellan head** and **Frik head** — on the same screen | pick one |

**One word, two meanings:** *Model* (which droid vs which mesh — both inside the
Model tab) · *Build/Builder* (mechanism editor vs board compiler vs "your MK4
build") · *Frame* (a timeline frame vs a camera verb, adjacent in one row) ·
*Setup* (the wizard vs the measuring tool) · *Learn* (a stage toggle vs a sidebar
tab) · *Sequence* (a library entry vs a board slot, two lists with different
contents).

**Acronyms and units shown with no explanation anywhere:** `MK4 · CAD · tris ·
draw calls · .r2m · .mstr · .ino · sequences.h · servos.h · PCA9685 · I2C · PWM ·
UART · ADK · Mega 2560 · DY-SV5W · MD-YX5300 · Syren10 · Sabertooth 2x25 · FSESC ·
hub motors · AstroPixels · Teeces · PSI · HP · restartScript(0)…(7) · sub · 49f ·
Hz · ms · µs · quarter-µs · 0.50 (unlabelled) · PEN +2s · mm/Z-up → m/Y-up ·
BSD-3-Clause · SPD 1 · 30`.

And the community words a first-day builder does not have: **the sketch**
(≈10 minutes to work out it means the Arduino program), **pie**, **breadpan**,
**brick**, **loadout**, **throw**, **endpoints**, **travel**, **ease**,
**release**, **boot**, **rigged**, **kiosk**, **choreography**, **puppet the
servos**, **Sabertooth watchdog starvation** ("sounds like my droid is going to
die").

# §7 Layout, scale and responsiveness

### 7.1 Stage buttons unreachable — BLOCKER ✅
At 800×600, `Follow` (x = −159), `Grid` (−92) and `Reset pose` (−39) sit at
negative x with nothing to scroll them back — and they are the only way to
recover when the orbit camera loses the droid. At the **default** 1440×900 in the
`SEQUENCE` workspace, `Follow` renders at x = 863 under a pane starting at 950:
invisible and unclickable with no resizing at all.

**Fix:** `overflow-x:auto` on the toolbar, clamped to the stage pane's own box,
collapsing to an overflow button below ~900 px.

### 7.2 The rest of the scale failures ✅ (three of them)

| Condition | What breaks |
|---|---|
| 1440×900 @ 100% | All six chips truncated ✅ |
| 1440×900 @ 115% / 150% | Chips lose all text; "R2-D2" clipped off the header ✅ |
| 1024×700 / 800×600 | Chips become anonymous dots ✅ |
| Splitter dragged, any scale ≠ 100% | **The pane edge runs away from the pointer** by the zoom factor, and the clamp is in the wrong space so the sidebar can swallow the stage ✅ |
| Stage pickers / fault popover at any scale ≠ 100% | **Land far from their button, often off-screen** — 360 px right and 200 px below at 150% ✅ |
| Splitter dragged right | Firmware chip clipped mid-word, no ellipsis |
| Splitter dragged up | DOME card overlaps the stage edge; orbit hint collides with the toolbar |
| Splitter dragged down | Virtual pad shrinks to a 90 px thumbnail, no minimum |
| A− / A+ | Range is asymmetric — 90 % to 150 %. 90 % is the only size where the header was fully readable |

### 7.3 Two headers on screen at once — MAJOR
The Panels / Colours / Scene jobs narrow the setup overlay so the droid is
visible beside it — and leave the whole app header exposed and clickable at
top-right, inches from the overlay's own `📖 MANUAL | DARK | LIGHT | CLOSE`. The
`DRIVE` tab is cut to "VE". And the page's claim — *"The droid is beside you —
press ▶ to prove the travel"* — was false: the stage showed no droid.

# §8 Help, and the map

### 8.1 The manual opens a blank tab — BLOCKER
The biggest, bluest button on the first panel a new user reads, promising
*"Twenty-one chapters and eight clips on getting a real droid moving"*, opens a
tab that never resolves. No error, no fallback, no message. The small print
("it needs a connection at the moment you click") is in the sidebar only, not on
the header button — and in a garage with no wifi the app's only documentation
silently does not exist.

**Fix:** probe first, and replace the caption in place with *"The manual lives
online — download it once from the Releases page while you have a connection."*
Better: ship it inside the HTML.

### 8.2 Three keyboard legends, none of which mentions arming
The always-visible `KEYBOARD` table and the `?` card are near-identical lists;
the `PRIMARY` table is a different list in the other direction. To learn "Enter
arms the feet" you must read two of them and join them yourself.
**Fix:** put the meaning in the `?` card and delete the duplicate table.

### 8.3 The map a newcomer has to hold
Five separate tab strips (workspaces, right pane, lower pane, Config sub-tabs,
Track Builder modes), three levels of nesting, and about a dozen modal surfaces
that are places in their own right.

```
SETUP overlay  — 9 questions + 6 "jobs"          [essential once]
HEADER         — 6 status pills · 4 workspaces · Setup · Manual · ? · Menu
  DRIVE        — stage + toolbar; lower tabs Controller|Sequencer;
                 right tabs Controls|Outputs|Learn        [essential]
  SEQUENCE     — timeline, parts, ready-made, library, build   [advanced]
  CONFIGURE    — Config (Sketch|Panels|Files) · Model (5 models, Builder,
                 part card, groups, alignment)            [essential, 3 deep]
  BOARD        — servo/sequence config · outputs · Advanced console  [advanced]
SIM ONLY       — an entire alternate shell
overlays       — Track Builder · shortcuts card · Credits · Save & load ·
                 servo bench · dome map · part inspector · channel inspector
```

**Essential for a first-timer:** Setup, Drive, Configure ▸ Config. **Fold away
until needed:** Sequence, Board, the Advanced checkbox, the Builder and
Servo-gauges models.

# §9 Everything else, briefly

**Model Builder.** The premise is unavailable on the firmware the setup
recommends — the CHANNELS panel says joints cannot be wired on mod2026, its
"Open the setup — Firmware" button deep-links correctly, and then a **second**
wall appears with no button, naming a *"Servo tab"* that does not exist.
Switching to it shows a giant untextured grey slab (the base plate at the R2-D2
camera framing) with no grid drawn, while the sidebar still shows the R2-D2
sound bank and button map. Units contradict the blurb (50 mm vs `0.05 m`). A
hinge's sockets are called `flag` and `flag tip`. The `0 OF 12` limit is
explained only inside a collapsed disclosure *below* the thing it explains — a
disclosure which is, itself, excellent.

**Track Builder.** No plain **Save** — the only exits are `SAVE AS NEW`, `SAVE AS
A COPY`, `RENAME`, `DELETE`, `RESET TO DEFAULT` and `CANCEL`, and Cancel discards
a dirty curve silently. The DRAW / GATES / CONES tabs are pixel-identical apart
from which one is highlighted. The live validator, though, is genuinely
excellent. Track mode itself starts you disarmed, off-camera and already
accruing `PEN +2s`.

**Servo gauges.** `ch 0 / Servo 1` … `ch 23 / Servo 24` — two numbering schemes
for the same thing, printed one above the other.

**Kiosk.** The containment is genuinely tight: after entering, exactly **one**
interactive element exists, and 46 keys and combinations reached nothing new.
Three real problems, all ✅ fixed in v1.69.0 — a stranger could click the droid
and get the full part editor including the channel re-map; a wrong password gave
no feedback at all; and letters typed at the unlock prompt drove the droid
(DRIVE 29 → 37, SPD 1 → SPD 2). **Still open:** kiosk opens with whatever camera
the operator left behind — one walkthrough's public view had no droid in it at
all — and there is no Follow, Reset pose or Front button, so the first visitor to
spin the mouse wheel ends the exhibit until an operator types the password.

**PCA Studio** is a second, separate app with a pixel-identical copy of the
six-step servo wizard and its own project format, and nothing tells a person what
it is, how it relates, or which of the two they should be using.

# §10 What is genuinely good

Said plainly, because it is unusual and it should survive any redesign.

1. **The writing.** *"attach to is the point of the whole thing: put a part on
   another one and it becomes part of it."* *"Press START. Nothing drives until
   you do — that is deliberate, and it is the same on the real droid."* Each
   teaches in one sentence. The tone is consistent and never condescending.
2. **The confirmations are a masterclass.** `CLEAR EVERY BRICK?` names the count,
   names the target, says what survives, and tells you the escape hatch, with
   `KEEP THEM` / `CLEAR IT` as verbs rather than Yes/No. `RESET EVERYTHING` adds
   *"Setup .json files you have saved to disk are NOT touched"* and calls its
   button `WIPE AND RESTART`. This is the standard the rest of the app should be
   held to — and the gap between these and the unguarded destructive paths is
   most of §5.
3. **The not-applicable handling.** When the model was a wheeled chariot, two
   questions became a plain-English amber banner — *"Not used by the Polar Mouse
   + chariot … Your answer is kept for the droid and nothing here is lost"* —
   with a button back. Better than 90 % of setup wizards, and the pattern §1.7
   and §1.8 should copy.
4. **Q9 narrows the firmware list to what the hardware can run** and prints the ✕
   reason under each blocked option, with a one-click recommendation.
5. **The photographs are real.** The actual Sabertooth, the actual PCA9685, the
   actual receiver. For someone matching a board in their hand to a name on a
   forum that is the most useful thing on the screen.
6. **The physical-job checklist.** *"Power the board from its own 5–6 V supply.
   Join the grounds. Fit the horn with the servo commanded to centre. Creep up on
   each endpoint and stop at the touch."* Closing with *"Three things that break
   servos, every time"*. Better than most forum posts — it just needs the UI to
   enforce it (§2.1).
7. **Refusing to draw power distribution, and saying why.** *"No V+ lines are
   drawn, on purpose."* Right call, well argued.
8. **`READY-MADE`.** One click produced a proper staggered seven-brick dome wave.
   Once found, it is the whole product.
9. **The brick inspector's judgement**: `RUNS FOR` in seconds, `OPENS TO` in %,
   and `MOTION` as *"Opens, then closes"* rather than a keyframe editor.
10. **Undo works and is trustworthy** in the sequencer, and unsaved edits survive
    switching away and back.
11. **Empty states that say what to do next**, with a primary button.
12. **The dome map and the Panels job** are exactly the right shape for their
    jobs — droid beside you, ▶ to prove the travel.
13. **The `.mstr` file carries a warning in its own header** telling you the
    values are placeholders and to verify at low speed.
14. **Kiosk containment** (see §9) and **splitter clamping** are both properly
    done.
