MAESTROPCA BENCH TEST CARD

Ten slots, one per feature · 7 servos across two PCA9685s · flash sequences.h + MaestroReplacement

Every feature we built past the Maestro gets one slot, and each slot is written so the thing it proves is visible from across the bench rather than buried in a serial log. Open the Serial Monitor at 115200 (or PCA Studio's monitor panel), press the number, watch the servo.

Endpoints are deliberately narrow. 5000–7000 quarter-µs is 1250–1750 µs, home 6000 — a ±250 µs throw. Nothing in these tests can drive a horn into a stop, and every servo has the same travel so differences you see are the feature, not the linkage.

The ten slots

0Parkall seven
Do
Press 0 before and after every other test.
Watch for
All seven servos ease to centre together, no jerk, no buzz.
Proves
Every channel is alive and both boards are awake. This is your baseline.
If wrong
A servo that doesn't move is wiring or a dead board — press ? and check both 0x40 and 0x41 say FOUND, then w to walk each channel one at a time.
1Sweepch0 · background oscillator
Do
Press 1. Leave it running — it never stops on its own.
Watch for
ch0 sweeping smoothly end to end on a 3 second cycle, turning around without stopping dead at either end.
Proves
The oscillator generator, and that a generator costs 5 words of flash instead of a frame table.
If wrong
A hard stop at the ends means the trapezoid isn't being applied. Twitching means the I2C bus is struggling — check pull-ups and the 400 kHz setting.
2Alternatech1 ch2 ch3 · loops
Do
With 1 still sweeping, press 2.
Watch for
ch1 and ch3 up while ch2 goes down, back and forth forever — and ch0 never breaks stride.
Proves
Concurrency. Two sequences on disjoint channels run at once; this is the thing the Padawan servo loops could not do.
If wrong
If pressing 2 stops the sweep, the channel masks are overlapping when they shouldn't.
3Ease comparech1 ch2 ch3 · loops
Do
Press 0, then 3. Watch the three horns side by side for several cycles.
Watch for
ch1 (none) starts immediately. ch2 (soft) visibly hangs back for the first fraction of a second, then catches up. ch3 (overshoot) keeps up with ch1 but goes ~25 µs past the target and settles back — a small, deliberate nod at the end of the move.
Proves
All three easing profiles, on identical hardware with identical speed and acceleration.
If wrong
Three identical moves means the ease column didn't survive generation — check the header rows say MPCA_EASE_SOFT and MPCA_EASE_OVERSHOOT.
Why this one targets 6600, not 7000. Overshoot aims past the target, and the engine clamps at the calibrated maximum — so a move that already targets the endpoint has nowhere to overshoot into and comes out identical to none. Simulating this test is what caught that; the slot now moves inside the range so the character is actually visible.
4Wanderch15 · background
Do
Press 0, then 4. Watch for a full minute.
Watch for
ch15 drifting to unpredictable positions at unpredictable moments — never a pattern, never the same twice, never leaving its range.
Proves
The wander generator, and that the last pin on the first board works.
If wrong
A repeating pattern means the RNG is being reseeded. Dead means pin 15 — try w.
5Release A/Bch16 vs ch31 · the audible one
Do
Press 5. Listen rather than look. Put a finger lightly on each horn afterwards.
Watch for
Both go out, both come back. Then, about 1.2 s after ch16 stops moving, it goes silent and limp — you can turn it by hand. ch31 keeps its faint holding buzz and resists.
Proves
releaseMs: a parked panel that draws no current and makes no noise. The Maestro cannot do this.
If wrong
If ch16 never goes quiet, the last frame is too short to outlast travel plus the release timer. If it goes limp and drops, that channel is holding against gravity and must keep releaseMs 0.
6Cross board waveall seven, in order
Do
Press 6.
Watch for
A wave: 0, 1, 2, 3, 15, then 16 and 31 on the second board, then everything home.
Proves
Both I2C addresses are being written, and channel→board→pin mapping is right end to end. This is the test that caught channels 16 and 31 being dead.
If wrong
A wave that stops after ch15 means board 0x41 never woke — check the A0 jumper is bridged and that ? lists 0x41.
7Grab ch0the important one
Do
Press 1 to start the sweep. Wait until it's clearly running. Then press 7.
Watch for
The sweep stops, ch0 makes one deliberate out-and-back gesture — and then starts sweeping again by itself, with nothing pressed.
Proves
Background layering: an idle animation is displaced by a real gesture and resumes when the gesture releases the channel. This is the behaviour that makes a droid look alive between commands.
If wrong
If the sweep doesn't come back, the background flag was lost in generation — the header line for slot 1 must read MPCA_SEQ_BACKGROUND | MPCA_SEQ_OSC.
8Dual sweepch16 + ch31 · 90° apart
Do
Press 0, then 8.
Watch for
Both second-board servos sweeping the same 2.6 s cycle but a quarter cycle out of step — when one is at an end, the other is at mid-travel.
Proves
Phase offset within one generator sequence, and two boards animating simultaneously rather than in turn.
If wrong
In lockstep means the phase column was dropped; check the header's five-number generator rows.
9All homethe way out
Do
Press 9 to finish, or x to stop everything and switch all outputs off.
Watch for
Everything back to centre, quietly.
Proves
Nothing new — it's the reset between runs.

Console keys you'll want alongside

  • ? status — I2C scan (both 0x40 and 0x41 must say FOUND), channel and slot counts, what the host last sent.
  • w walk — nudges every configured channel in turn. The fastest way to find one dead servo.
  • t sweep ch0 — proves servo and power without any sequence at all.
  • v verbose — logs every command arriving over the Maestro link. When the droid's host is wired in and nothing happens, this tells you whether the bytes ever arrived.
  • x off — stop everything, all outputs off.
Before you power up. V+ on both PCA9685s from the 5–6 V servo supply, never the Arduino's 5 V pin. Grounds common between supply, Arduino and both boards. Seven servos moving at once is several amps.
