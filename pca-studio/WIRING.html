The four things that bite

Never power servos from the Arduino

A servo's stall current is amps. Taking V+ from the Arduino's 5 V pin browns out the regulator, the MCU resets mid-move, and the droid twitches or runs away. V+ gets its own supply, always — this is the single most common way a first PCA9685 build fails.

Common ground is not optional

The servo supply's negative must tie to the Arduino's GND. Without it the PWM signal has no shared reference: servos jitter, buzz, or ignore you entirely — and it looks exactly like a broken sketch.

Calibrate the oscillator

The PCA9685's internal RC clock is only nominally 25 MHz and drifts a few percent board to board. Until you set the measured value — the osc box in PCA Studio, or maestro.begin(26400000UL) — your quarter-µs endpoints are approximate on the wire, and your hard-won Maestro calibration lands slightly off.

Fit the bulk capacitor

The Adafruit breakout has a footprint for it. ~1000 µF across V+ and GND absorbs the inrush when several servos start together; without it the rail sags and the whole bus glitches.

Addressing — and your existing I2C bus

  • 0x40 is the default with no jumpers bridged. Bridge A0 for 0x41, A1 for 0x42, and so on — six address bits, up to 62 boards on one bus.
  • Your 18-channel dome needs two boards — 16 + 2. The generated sequences.h already assumes this: channel i → board i/16, pin i%16, so ch0–15 are 0x40 and ch16–17 are 0x41.
  • No clash with your Padawan bus. Your triggerI2C() devices are decimal 10, 25, 26 and 27 — that's 0x0A, 0x19, 0x1A, 0x1B. Nowhere near 0x40.
  • One to watch: the PCA9685 also answers the "All Call" address 0x70 out of the box. If you ever add a device at 0x70, disable All Call first.
  • Chaining: on the real breakout you go right-hand header of board 1 → left-hand header of board 2, carrying all four lines. The diagram stacks them for clarity.

Option B — the co-processor (true Maestro replacement)

Everything above stays exactly as drawn; the PCA9685 just answers to a dedicated Nano instead of the droid's main board. The host sends two bytes and forgets — a different CPU runs the choreography, so panels stay smooth even when the main sketch is busy. Your Padawan sketch needs no changes: same PololuMaestro library, same restartScript(n), same single wire.

Bench rig vs standalone droid

Bench — PCA Studio live mode

Flash PCA_Bridge.ino. The wiring above is complete as drawn. The browser runs the sequencer and streams changed channel positions over USB at 115200; the Arduino is a dumb pipe to setPWM. Move a slider, a servo moves.

Nothing else needs to be connected — no receiver, no motor controllers, no audio.

Droid — standalone

Flash your Padawan sketch with MaestroPCA + the exported sequences.h. The PC and its USB link go away; everything else on this diagram stays identical.

Added back on the droid: USB Host Shield for the Xbox receiver, Sabertooth on Serial1, Syren on Serial2, DY-SV5W audio. Serial3 is now free — the Maestro's UART is what you're replacing.

First light — the order that finds faults fastest

  • Supply alone first. PSU on, meter across V+ and −, confirm 5–6 V before a servo is anywhere near it.
  • One servo, channel 0. Not eighteen. A single servo on a known channel turns "nothing works" into one testable claim.
  • Bridge sketch, no app. Open the serial monitor at 115200 — it prints PCA-BRIDGE 1 on boot. If that line is missing, the problem is the Arduino, not the wiring or the app.
  • Then PCA Studio. Connect hardware, drag channel 0's slider. If the bar moves in the browser but the servo doesn't, it's the I2C side or servo power — the app and the engine are already proven.
  • Endpoints last. Only once it moves at all: set min/max at low speed, watching for the linkage binding before you trust the numbers.
